Maryland’s freeze-thaw cycles, summer humidity, and UV exposure are hard on outdoor wood. At some point every deck owner faces the same question: **Is it time to repair, or should we replace the whole thing?**

## Signs a Repair Makes Sense

- Damage is localized (a few boards, one section of railing, or a single joist)
- The ledger board and primary beams are still solid
- The deck is less than 15–20 years old and was built to code
- Repair cost is well under half the price of a comparable new deck

In these cases, targeted board replacement, sistering a joist, or rebuilding a railing section can restore safety and appearance for years.

## Signs Replacement Is Usually Better

- Soft or rotten framing members throughout the structure
- Ledger board separation or missing/failed flashing
- Widespread insect damage
- Outdated construction that no longer meets current safety expectations
- Repair quotes approaching the cost of a new build

When the structure itself is compromised, continuing to patch surface problems only delays the inevitable and can leave a hidden safety risk.
