Living in Gaithersburg means dealing with four distinct seasons — and each one stresses outdoor wood differently.

## Winter: Freeze-Thaw Cycles

Water seeps into cracks, freezes, expands, and widens those cracks. Over successive winters this leads to checking, splitting, and eventually soft spots. Keeping a solid sealant film is the best defense.

## Spring: Moisture and Mold

Rising temperatures and frequent rain create ideal conditions for mildew and early-stage rot. A thorough cleaning and inspection in early spring catches problems while they are still inexpensive to fix.

## Summer: UV and Heat

Intense sunlight breaks down lignin in wood and fades finishes. High temperatures also open pores, letting moisture in later. UV-blocking stains and sealers dramatically slow this process.

## Fall: Preparation Window

Fall is the ideal time for staining or sealing. Cooler temperatures allow products to penetrate properly, and the deck is protected before winter arrives.

## Simple Maintenance Rhythm

1. **Early spring** — Clean, inspect, address soft boards or loose railings.
2. **Late spring / early summer** — Apply stain or sealer if needed.
3. **Fall** — Quick inspection and touch-up of high-wear areas.
4. **Anytime** — Fix safety issues (loose railings, soft spots) immediately.

A modest annual investment in cleaning and sealing almost always costs less than major repairs later.
